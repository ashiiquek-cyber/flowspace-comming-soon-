The project is envisioned on an expansive 20,000 sq. ft. west-facing, lakefront site, with a brief to accommodate a 180-seat brewery complemented by private hut-style seating spaces. The client sought a distinctive, theme-driven architectural identity that would introduce a new experiential destination to the town.

Our design approach evolved from the site's strongest natural asset — its west-facing lake frontage. The primary objective was to capture the prevailing breeze, maximise visual connections with the water body, and create an immersive relationship between the built environment and the surrounding landscape.

Drawing inspiration from the organic geometry of water ripples, we developed a circular, ripple-inspired spatial concept, with landscaped green pockets seamlessly integrated between the built volumes. The concentric and offset circular geometries became the primary design language, enabling a harmonious segregation of functional spaces while maintaining visual and spatial continuity throughout the site.

A central performance podium forms the heart of the development, with the building levels gradually ascending in both directions, establishing a dynamic spatial hierarchy. The individual huts are strategically oriented towards this central podium, ensuring visual engagement with performances and shared activities.

At the south-western corner, a vertically articulated structure introduces a watchtower-like experience, complemented by a windmill-inspired leaf-like structural element that enhances the project's visual identity.

The overall design philosophy revolves around the segmentation of the site through ripple-inspired circular geometries, radiating from a common focal point to generate a series of interconnected and offset architectural volumes. The built forms are strategically shaped with wide, mouth-like openings oriented towards the prevailing south-western breeze, encouraging natural ventilation and allowing the wind to permeate the interior spaces.

Ultimately, the design seeks to transform the lakefront into an experiential architectural destination where landscape, wind, water, geometry, and social interaction converge into a unified spatial experience.

## Material and surface

The material palette is primarily driven by prefabricated construction components, including metal pipes and metal decking sheets, ensuring construction efficiency while complementing the project's distinctive architectural language. Mild steel is also extensively incorporated to create bespoke design elements and enhance the structural and aesthetic expression of the building.

The flooring combines Moroccan-patterned vitrified tiles with black granite slab borders, creating a distinctive visual contrast and defining the individual spatial zones. The landscaped areas are integrated with cobblestone paving, arranged in varied patterns and organic compositions that complement the ripple-inspired design concept.

Polycarbonate sheets are incorporated as lightweight architectural elements, allowing filtered natural light into the spaces. Patterned, glossy-finish tiles are also used to introduce decorative textures and visual richness to selected surfaces.

The individual hut structures feature single-slope roofing finished with green-toned shingles, establishing a consistent architectural character across the private seating zones. Landscaped pockets throughout the development are enriched with lush vegetation, ornamental plants, and cascading creepers, softening the structural elements and creating a seamless transition between the built environment and nature.

The overall material expression is unified through a carefully curated combination of olive green and golden tones, enhancing the project's visual identity.

## Local context culture

he project is primarily driven by the site's specific microclimatic and contextual conditions rather than by conventional local architectural influences. The design evolves in response to the immediate site characteristics, particularly the lakefront orientation, prevailing wind patterns, views, and available open spaces.

In terms of functional requirements, the client specifically requested private hut-style seating arrangements to accommodate different user preferences. Recognizing the popularity of informal, dhaba-style seating experiences, we incorporated these spaces as an alternative dining option, offering a more relaxed, intimate, and casual atmosphere alongside the main brewery seating.

The intention was to create a diverse dining experience that responds to user behavior while maintaining a strong connection with the surrounding landscape.

## Design solution

The design responds to the site's irregular geometry by adopting an organic, ripple-inspired planning approach rather than imposing a rigid geometric layout. The circular and offset volumes are strategically arranged to follow the natural site configuration, allowing the architecture to integrate seamlessly with the available landscape while creating distinct yet interconnected activity zones.

The use of prefabricated construction elements enabled greater flexibility in shaping the curvilinear architectural forms while maintaining construction efficiency and material coordination. The roof profiles, floor slabs, structural columns, and pedestrian pathways were developed as a continuous design language, ensuring consistency between architectural expression and structural execution.

Climatic responsiveness was addressed through strategically oriented openings, interconnected green spaces, and open-to-sky zones that encourage natural cross-ventilation. The wide, lake-facing openings are designed to draw prevailing breezes into the development while maximizing views towards the water body and enhancing the overall outdoor experience.

Planning efficiency was achieved by establishing a clear circulation hierarchy that separates guest movement, service access, and operational activities. The kitchen was strategically positioned to facilitate uninterrupted all-day operations, with efficient connectivity to the dining and service areas without disturbing the guest experience.

The central performance podium acts as the primary spatial anchor, around which the brewery, private huts, and communal spaces are organized. The gradual variation in building levels and the orientation of individual huts towards the podium create visual connectivity and encourage social interaction.

Ultimately, the design transforms the challenges of an irregular site, prefabricated construction, and climatic considerations into opportunities for architectural expression. The result is an integrated lakefront destination where form, function, landscape, and environmental responsiveness work together to create a distinctive social experience.

## Design challenge

The irregular configuration of the site, with its non-geometric boundaries, presented an initial challenge in establishing a cohesive architectural language. Developing the built forms using prefabricated materials while maintaining an open, climate-responsive ambience required careful consideration of construction techniques, material selection, and spatial planning.

One of the primary planning challenges was to establish an efficient circulation network that ensured seamless movement, clear accessibility, and functional connectivity across the entire development. The positioning of the kitchen was particularly critical, as it needed to support uninterrupted, all-day operations while maintaining efficient service access and avoiding conflicts with the primary guest circulation.

Another significant design challenge was the consistent integration of curvilinear geometries across multiple architectural elements. Translating the ripple-inspired design language into the roof profiles, floor slabs, pedestrian pathways, and structural columns demanded careful coordination between architectural expression, structural feasibility, and construction methodology.

The challenge was not merely to introduce curves as a visual feature, but to establish a continuous architectural vocabulary in which every element contributed to the overall spatial identity. Balancing the expressive nature of the design with prefabrication constraints, climatic responsiveness, operational efficiency, and ease of construction became a fundamental part of the design development process.
